## Why the Wait Has Been So Long

The simplest explanation is also the most accurate: NetherRealm has been busy making Mortal Kombat games. The studio has historically alternated between its two flagship franchises rather than running them in parallel, and that rhythm explains most of the gap.

Look at the pattern. Injustice: Gods Among Us landed in 2013, Mortal Kombat X in 2015, Injustice 2 in 2017, and Mortal Kombat 11 in 2019. That was a brisk two-year cadence. Then things slowed. Mortal Kombat 11 received multiple expansions and next-gen versions into 2020, and Mortal Kombat 1 arrived in 2023, followed by a substantial story expansion. Add in the industry-wide shift to longer development cycles, remote-work disruption during the pandemic years, and corporate restructuring around WB Games, and you have a studio with a full plate.

A few practical factors keep the Injustice 3 conversation stuck in neutral:

- **One franchise at a time.** NetherRealm has never shipped a mainline Mortal Kombat and a mainline Injustice game in the same year.
- **Post-launch support eats years.** Modern fighting games are supported with seasons of DLC, balance patches, and expansions long after release.
- **Licensing adds complexity.** A DC game involves coordination with Warner Bros. Discovery's film, television, and comics arms — a heavier lift than an internally owned franchise.
- **Announcements come late.** NetherRealm tends to reveal games only a few months before launch, which means silence tells us very little.

Injustice 2 was revealed roughly eleven months before launch; Mortal Kombat 1 was revealed about four months before launch. That means a hypothetical Injustice 3 could go from "official reveal" to "in your hands" in under a year.

| Scenario | What It Would Mean for Fans | What We'd Need to See First |

The takeaway: **do not expect a long runway.** If Injustice 3 is real and far along, the gap between "it's official" and "it's out" may be shorter than most people assume. Conversely, the absence of an announcement does not prove the game isn't being worked on.

## Rumors, Leaks, and Community Reports

This is where caution matters most. Search long enough and you'll find "leaked" rosters, alleged internal documents, and voice-actor hints. Treat all of it as **community reports** rather than confirmed information.

What has actually happened, broadly:

- **Developer teases.** NetherRealm leadership has publicly acknowledged fan demand for the series over the years, sometimes through informal polls and social media engagement. Teasing is not confirmation.
- **Voice-actor chatter.** Cast members from prior games have occasionally hinted at projects, but such comments are usually vague and often misread.
- **Fabricated leaks.** Fake roster images and invented release dates spread quickly because the demand for news is so high. Most collapse under scrutiny within days.
- **Player experience.** Longtime Injustice 2 players will tell you the game's online population has thinned over time, which fuels the appetite for a sequel — but community enthusiasm isn't evidence of a release plan.

The rule of thumb: if a claim doesn't come with a trailer, an official press release, or a statement from Warner Bros. Games, file it as rumor.
