Two patterns stand out. First, the studio rarely ships two entries in the same franchise back to back. Second, the gap between major releases has stretched, not shrunk — modern fighting games carry longer post-launch support cycles, which delays whatever comes next.

If you apply the historical gap to the last release, a new NetherRealm title would land somewhere in the back half of the 2020s. Whether that title is Injustice 3 or another Mortal Kombat is the part nobody outside the studio can confirm.

### The Single Most Reliable Indicator

The clearest sign is not a leak or a rumor — it is the current game going quiet. When a studio stops selling new content for its live fighting game, the next project is usually deep in production. Player experience backs this up: the wind-down phase has preceded every major NetherRealm reveal of the past decade.

## What Could Shape the Sequel's Story and Roster

The biggest wildcard is not the calendar — it is DC itself. The film and television side of the brand has been rebooted, and that creates two competing pressures.

- **Fresh-start pressure:** A rebooted DC universe gives the studio freedom to redesign costumes, recast characters, and ignore older continuity.
- **Legacy pressure:** Injustice's identity is built on dark, alternate-timeline versions of familiar heroes, and fans expect that tone to survive.
- **Ending problem:** Injustice 2 shipped with two possible endings. Any sequel has to pick one, or write around both, which is exactly the kind of decision that slows a story down.

Guest fighters are another near-certainty. The previous game's roster leaned on crossovers, and a sequel would likely do the same to keep the marketing conversation alive long after launch.

### Platforms and Technical Expectations

A modern sequel would be built for current-generation consoles and PC from the start, with a likely simultaneous launch across platforms. Fighting games live or die on netcode, so expect rollback networking and a heavy emphasis on online stability. None of that is confirmed, but it is the baseline the genre now demands.
