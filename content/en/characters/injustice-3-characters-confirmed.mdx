## The Short Answer: Nobody Has Been Confirmed

If you went looking for **Injustice 3 characters confirmed** by an official source, you ran into the same wall as everyone else: there is nothing official to find. NetherRealm Studios has never announced a third Injustice game, and that means no fighter, stage, or story beat has been formally confirmed either. Every **Injustice 3 characters confirmed** list you see online is built on rumor, datamining, wishful thinking, or plain clickbait.

That matters because the gap between games keeps growing. Injustice: Gods Among Us launched in 2013, Injustice 2 followed in 2017, and after its final DLC wave wrapped in 2018, the studio pivoted to Mortal Kombat. Fans have spent years reading tea leaves ever since.

This guide separates what is actually known from what is merely hoped for, so you can stop chasing fake roster reveals.

## Where the Roster Conversation Actually Stands

Ed Boon, the creative director behind both Injustice and Mortal Kombat, has been asked about a third Injustice game for years. His answers tend to be playful rather than definitive — a wink, a cryptic poll, a "we'll see" — which is exactly the kind of response that fuels endless speculation. It is not a confirmation, and treating it as one is how fake rosters spread.

What is clear is that NetherRealm has been busy elsewhere. After Injustice 2, the studio shipped Mortal Kombat 11 and then Mortal Kombat 1, each with their own lengthy DLC cycles. Community reports have also circulated about NetherRealm working on projects outside the Injustice brand, though none of those reports have been confirmed by the studio or its publisher.

In other words: the developer has not closed the door, but nobody has walked through it either.

## How to Tell a Real Confirmation From Clickbait

The fastest way to avoid being fooled is to check the source type before you read the headline. A genuine roster reveal comes from the developer or publisher, usually through a trailer, a press release, or a major gaming outlet reporting on one of those. Anything else is secondhand at best.

Community reports are not worthless — datamining has correctly predicted content before — but they are unverified until someone official says otherwise. Treat them as leads, not facts.

| Source Type | Reliability | How to Verify |
|---|---|---|
| Developer or publisher announcement | Highest | Check official channels |
| Major gaming press | High | Look for a named source |
| Datamining leaks | Medium | Wait for corroboration |
| YouTube "roster reveal" videos | Low | Check description for sources |
| AI-written listicles | Very low | Look for zero citations |

One practical habit helps: if an article claims Injustice 3 characters confirmed but never names who confirmed them, close the tab. Real news always has a "who said it."

## FAQ

**Are any Injustice 3 characters confirmed right now?**
No. As of today, there is no officially announced Injustice 3, so there is no confirmed roster. Any list claiming otherwise is speculation presented as fact.

**Has Ed Boon ever confirmed Injustice 3?**
He has acknowledged fan interest many times and made teasing comments, but he has never issued a formal confirmation that the game is in development.

**Which characters are most likely to return if a sequel happens?**
Batman, Superman, and Wonder Woman are the safest assumptions, followed by core Justice League members and Harley Quinn. Everything beyond that is community guesswork.

**Where should I look for real announcements?**
Official NetherRealm or publisher channels, plus established gaming outlets reporting directly from them. Avoid aggregator sites that cite no source.

**Should I trust leaked rosters?**
Be skeptical. Some leaks have been accurate in the past, but the overwhelming majority of "Injustice 3 characters confirmed" claims are fabricated for clicks.

Until an official reveal happens, the healthiest approach is to enjoy the speculation without mistaking it for news. The roster will be real when the studio says it is — and not one minute before.
