Few sequels in fighting-game history carry as much narrative baggage as the one fans keep asking about: the **Injustice 3 plot**. The first two games turned DC's greatest heroes into tyrants, rebels, and grieving parents, and the sequel closed on a choice that split its timeline in half. That is exactly why the Injustice 3 plot matters so much — whatever NetherRealm decides to canonize will retroactively decide who was right, who was punished, and which version of Batman and Superman the next generation of players inherits.

This guide walks through what the games actually left behind, which story threads are still dangling, and how to separate real signals from wishful thinking while waiting for news.

## The Two Injustice 2 Endings That Any Injustice 3 Plot Must Resolve

Injustice 2 doesn't end with a single cutscene — it ends with a decision. In the final chapter, you fight as either Batman or Superman, and whichever side wins becomes the closing scene. That means any future Injustice 3 plot has to pick a lane, or find a clever way to honor both outcomes without gutting the emotional payoff of either.

Here's how the two branches differ, and what each one hands to a sequel.

| Ending | What the final scene shows | What it sets up for a sequel |
|---|---|---|
| Batman's ending | Superman is defeated and contained in a red-sun cell beneath the Fortress of Solitude; Batman begins rebuilding a League that answers to no single leader | A fragile, demilitarized world where the Regime's loyalists are still free and angry |
| Superman's ending | Superman sends Batman into the Phantom Zone and reasserts the Regime, with the Justice League reborn as an instrument of his will | A resistance story told from the losing side, with Batman absent from the board |

The tie-in comic *Injustice vs. Masters of the Universe* picked up the Superman ending, which makes it the closest thing to a soft canon the franchise has ever offered. That said, the games themselves have never formally declared a winner — and community reports have argued both ways for years.

### Why the choice matters more than the villain

NetherRealm's story modes usually hinge on a threat, not a verdict. Injustice 2 flipped that. The villain — Brainiac — is defeated in both versions, but the *aftermath* is the real cliffhanger. A sequel has to decide whether Superman's imprisonment held, or whether it was always going to fail.

## Loose Threads and Characters Most Likely to Return

Even if you ignore the ending split, Injustice 2 leaves a remarkable number of open doors. Several major characters end the story with unresolved loyalties, and a few of them are exactly the kind of wildcards a new story mode would want.

| Character | Where Injustice 2 leaves them | Why they matter going forward |
|---|---|---|
| Superman | Imprisoned (Batman ending) or ruling (Superman ending) | He is the franchise's central question, not just its final boss |
| Batman | Rebuilding the League, or trapped in the Phantom Zone | His methods are the counterargument to everything Superman does |
| Wonder Woman | Survives as a committed Regime loyalist | She gives the Regime a face after Superman is contained |
| Damian Wayne | Caught between his father and his Regime loyalties | The cleanest "next generation" arc the series has built |
| Harley Quinn | Allied with Batman after breaking from the Joker's shadow | A redemption thread that pays off in a resistance storyline |
| Supergirl | Her allegiance depends on player choices during the story | An easy way to write a hero who can stand beside or against Superman |
| Brainiac | Defeated, but his ship and collected cities remain | A ready-made cosmic threat that doesn't require a new villain |
| Ra's al Ghul | Beaten, but his ideology and stockpiles survive him | The "cure the world by force" argument outlives the man |

Two of those threads stand out. First, Brainiac's collection never fully gets resolved — bottled cities and stolen knowledge are exactly the kind of loose end a sequel can pull. Second, the younger heroes (Damian, Supergirl, Blue Beetle, Firestorm) give the writers a way to pass the torch without retiring Clark and Bruce entirely.

Player experience suggests most fans want the Regime story continued rather than rebooted, though opinions split sharply on whether Superman should be redeemed or permanently contained.

## The Injustice Comics Are the Biggest Untapped Story Blueprint

If you want a preview of what an Injustice 3 plot could look like, the comics are the best place to look. The tie-in series ran for years and covered ground the games skipped entirely, including the five-year fall of Superman and a period piece about the Justice Society.

| Comic series | What it covers | Adaptable ideas for a game |
|---|---|---|
| *Year One* through *Year Five* | The slow collapse of the Regime's morality and Batman's insurgency | A story-mode structure that shows a regime rotting from the inside |
| *Ground Zero* | The first game retold from Harley Quinn's point of view | A side-story mode with a non-hero narrator |
| *Injustice 2* prequel comics | Batman's uneasy alliance with a returning Superman, plus the rise of the Society | Faction politics and betrayals between fights |
| *Injustice vs. Masters of the Universe* | A crossover that continues the Superman ending | A multiverse collision as a sequel premise |
| *Injustice: Year Zero* | A historical prequel about the Justice Society era | Flashback chapters that explain why the Regime's logic exists |

Comics also give the games something they've never had: downtime. The panels let characters argue, grieve, and change between battles — and a story mode that borrows that pacing would feel far less like a sequence of arenas.

## Possible Premises for a New Injustice Story

Nobody outside the studio knows the real direction, so treat every pitch below as informed speculation built from what the games and comics actually set up. Each premise has different strengths and different costs.

| Premise | What it would require | How well it fits the established story |
|---|---|---|
| Multiverse collision | Two Earths, two Batmen, one collapsing timeline | High — the Superman ending and the crossover comic both point here |
| Cosmic return | Brainiac's leftovers, Darkseid, or a larger collector threat | High — keeps the scale the series is known for |
| Regime restoration | A jailed Superman, a resurgent loyalist movement, Batman outnumbered | Medium-high — depends on which ending becomes canon |
| New generation | Damian, Supergirl, and Blue Beetle carrying the story | Medium — risky commercially, strong narratively |
| Timeline reset | A Flashpoint-style rewrite that merges both endings | Medium — solves the canon problem but undoes years of setup |

The safest creative bet is probably a hybrid: open with the Regime contained, let a cosmic or multiversal threat force the heroes to free Superman, then make the player live with the consequences of that alliance. It's the same emotional engine that made Injustice 2 work.

## Rumors vs. Confirmed: How to Read Injustice 3 Plot Talk

Search for news long enough and you'll find confident claims about a game that, as of this writing, has not been officially announced. Here's a practical way to sort the noise.

| Claim you'll see online | Status | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| "Injustice 3 has been announced" | Not confirmed by the studio or publisher | Announcements come with trailers, not forum posts |
| "NetherRealm's next project is Injustice 3" | Unconfirmed | The studio has alternated franchises before, but never on a public schedule |
| "Ed Boon teased it on social media" | True in spirit — he teases often | Teasing is not announcing; Boon has joked about many projects |
| "Leaked roster and story images" | Community reports, frequently fan-made | Fake story slides are one of the most common hoaxes in fighting-game communities |
| "The Superman ending is canon" | Supported by a tie-in comic, not by the games | Comics have never strictly bound the games' continuity |

### How to catch up on the story before any sequel

If you want to be ready the moment a new game is revealed, do this:

- **Play both Injustice 2 endings.** They take minutes and completely change your read on the story.
- **Read *Year One* and the Injustice 2 prequel comics.** They explain the Regime's decline better than any cutscene.
- **Watch the story-mode cinematics as a movie.** A full watch-through is the fastest way to memorize who betrayed whom.
- **Follow official channels only.** The [NetherRealm Studios official website](https://www.netherrealm.com/) is where real news lands first; anything else is speculation until it appears there.
- **Keep a mental timeline.** Injustice, Injustice 2, the comics, and the crossover all sit on one branching continuity.

## FAQ: Injustice 3 Plot Questions Answered

**Has Injustice 3 been confirmed?**
Not officially. As of now, there is no studio-confirmed announcement, release window, or story synopsis. Everything circulating online about the Injustice 3 plot is either speculation, community reporting, or based on threads left in the previous games and comics.

**Which Injustice 2 ending is canon for a sequel?**
Neither has been formally declared canon by the games. The crossover comic *Injustice vs. Masters of the Universe* follows the Superman ending, which is the strongest hint available — but a game could still choose the Batman branch, or write a premise that makes both endings matter.

**Do I need to read the comics to understand the Injustice 3 plot?**
No, but it helps. The games explain enough on their own, while the comics fill in the years between the two titles and give context to characters like Damian Wayne, Harley Quinn, and Ra's al Ghul who carry unresolved arcs into any sequel.

**Who would be the main villain in a new Injustice story?**
The most likely candidates are a returning cosmic threat tied to Brainiac's leftovers, a Regime loyalist movement led by Wonder Woman, or a multiversal antagonist that forces the two Earths into conflict. A hero-versus-hero conflict, rather than a single villain, remains the series' signature move — and the safest bet for whatever comes next.
