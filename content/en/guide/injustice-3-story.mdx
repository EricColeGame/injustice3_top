Ask any fighting-game fan what they want next from NetherRealm Studios and you will hear one answer more than any other: the Injustice 3 story. That is because the Injustice 3 story has to resolve a cliffhanger that Injustice 2 left pointing in two directions at once — and because nobody outside the studio knows which direction the developers plan to take.

Unlike a numbered sequel with a teaser trailer and a locked release date, this one lives in the space between rumor, leak, and wishful thinking. This guide separates what is actually known from what is assumed, walks through the plot threads most likely to carry forward, and gives you a practical roadmap for catching up before anything is officially announced.

## What We Actually Know About the Injustice 3 Story

NetherRealm Studios has never officially confirmed Injustice 3. That single fact shapes everything else here. There is no trailer, no release window, no verified roster, and no developer statement that locks in a direction for the Injustice 3 story. What does exist is a clear pattern: the studio alternates between its Mortal Kombat franchise and its DC branch, and the DC side has been quiet since 2017.

If you want to track announcements at the source, the [official NetherRealm Studios website](https://www.netherrealm.com/) is the place to watch for a reveal before anywhere else.

| Title | Release | Story Role |
|---|---|---|
| Mortal Kombat vs. DC Universe | 2008 | First DC crossover; not canon to the Injustice timeline |
| Injustice: Gods Among Us | 2013 | Establishes the Regime vs. Insurgency conflict |
| Injustice: Gods Among Us — Year One through Year Five (comics) | 2013–2016 | Fills the five-year gap before the first game |
| Injustice 2 | 2017 | Brainiac invasion; two branching endings |
| Injustice 2 (comic series) | 2017–2018 | Bridges the first game and the second |
| Injustice (animated film) | 2021 | Condensed adaptation of the Year One comic |

Community reports have circulated for years that a third installment was in some stage of development before the studio shifted focus to Mortal Kombat 1. Those reports have never been confirmed by the developer, so treat them as player experience and speculation rather than fact.

## Both Injustice 2 Endings, Explained

Injustice 2 does something unusual for a fighting game: it hands the player a final choice that changes who wins, and then declines to declare a winner. Both outcomes are presented as equally real, which means the Injustice 3 story has to eventually pick a lane — or find a clever way to honor both.

| Final Choice | What Happens | What It Sets Up |
|---|---|---|
| Batman's ending | Superman is imprisoned in the Phantom Zone; Batman and his allies begin rebuilding a fractured world | A contained Superman, a fragile peace, and an escape or redemption arc |
| Superman's ending | Superman imprisons Batman in the Phantom Zone and imposes a new order over Earth | A regime sequel where the heroes become the resistance |

Fans have argued about which ending should be canon since launch day. The honest answer is that neither has been declared official. NetherRealm has historically resolved branching endings in creative ways — sometimes by picking one, sometimes by folding multiple outcomes into a single timeline — so the door stays open.

If the studio follows its own habits, expect the next game to open with a short, wordless recap that establishes which world you are standing in before the first fight begins.

The rumor mill around this franchise is loud, and much of it is unsourced. Here is a grounded look at the claims that circulate most often, sorted by how much weight they deserve.

| Claim | Where It Comes From | How Much Weight It Deserves |
|---|---|---|

| NetherRealm's next project is a new IP entirely | Studio comments about wanting to try something different | Plausible |
| The story will connect Injustice and Mortal Kombat into one canon | Long-running crossover wish fulfillment | Very unlikely |

A few things are worth noting about how NetherRealm builds antagonists. The first game used a Superman who had gone too far. The second used an alien collector who reduced entire cities to bottles. Both threats were personal — they targeted characters the player already cared about rather than abstract cosmic danger.

That pattern suggests any future big bad will need a direct relationship with the existing cast, not just a bigger power level. A villain who has never met Batman or Wonder Woman is harder to sell as the emotional center of a story than one who has already betrayed them.

### How NetherRealm Usually Structures Its Third Acts

Most NetherRealm story modes follow a recognizable rhythm, and knowing it helps you predict where a sequel will spend its screen time.

| Act | Typical Beat | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | A returning threat reasserts itself | Establishes stakes before the player has a favorite |
| First act | Old allies fracture over how to respond | Forces player-versus-player fights that make sense |
| Midpoint | An uneasy alliance forms against a bigger enemy | Creates the game's best character pairings |
| Final act | A single choice splits the ending | Guarantees debate that lasts for years |

That structure is efficient, and it is almost certainly what a third entry would use. The question is which fracture line it picks: Superman versus Batman again, or a new split between the heroes who want to rebuild and the ones who want revenge.

## How to Catch Up Before the Next Game Drops

You do not need to read every tie-in to follow the Injustice 3 story, but a little homework pays off enormously. Here is the most efficient order for getting current.

- **Play Injustice: Gods Among Us (2013).** The original story mode is short and still explains the Regime's origin better than any summary.
- **Read the Year One through Year Five comics.** These cover the five-year gap and give the deepest context for why the heroes turned on each other.
- **Play Injustice 2 (2017).** Watch both endings — they take about ten minutes combined and are the single most important thing to understand.
- **Skim the Injustice 2 comic run.** It bridges the two games and introduces characters who never appeared on screen.
- **Watch the 2021 animated film if you want a fast refresher.** It condenses Year One into roughly 80 minutes.

| If You Have... | Do This |
|---|---|
| 2 hours | Watch both Injustice 2 endings and a story recap |
| 10 hours | Play Injustice 2's story mode start to finish |
| 30 hours | Play both games and read the Year One comic |
| Unlimited time | Full comic run plus both games plus the animated film |

One practical tip: play Injustice 2 with the story mode on the easiest difficulty. You are there for the cutscenes and the choice at the end, not for a challenge run. Nothing in the narrative is gated behind difficulty, and you can always replay chapters later on a harder setting.

## FAQ: Injustice 3 Story Questions Answered

**Is Injustice 3 officially confirmed?**
No. NetherRealm Studios has not announced a third game, and there is no verified release date, trailer, or roster. Anything you see claiming otherwise should be treated as a rumor until it appears on an official channel.

**Which Injustice 2 ending is canon for the Injustice 3 story?**
Neither has been declared canon. NetherRealm has a history of resolving branching endings in unexpected ways, so it is worth knowing both outcomes rather than assuming one is "the real" one.

**Do I need to read the comics to follow the Injustice 3 story?**
Not strictly. The games are written to stand alone, and each one recaps what came before. The comics simply add depth to character motivations, especially for the five-year gap between the first game's opening and its main events.

**Could the Injustice 3 story connect to Mortal Kombat?**
Almost certainly not as shared canon. Crossovers between the two franchises have happened as guest appearances and marketing events, but NetherRealm has kept their storylines in separate universes. Expect the DC timeline to stay self-contained.

The bottom line is simple: the Injustice 3 story is still unwritten, and that is exactly why it is worth following closely. The groundwork is laid, the endings are waiting, and the moment an announcement lands, everything above becomes a checklist instead of a guess.
