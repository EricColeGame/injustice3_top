Every major NetherRealm fighting game since 2013 has shipped with a mobile companion, and those companions are not stripped-down ports. They're built from scratch around collection, progression, and short-session combat.

| Title | Launch year | Platforms | Core loop |

Three patterns stand out, and they tell you a lot about what Injustice 3 Mobile would look like:

- **Day-one synergy.** The mobile game arrives alongside or near the console release, so both audiences promote each other.
- **Free-to-play with collection at the center.** You don't buy characters outright; you earn shards, unlock them, then chase gear.
- **Long tails.** These games get updated for years, not months. Injustice 2 Mobile is proof of that.

That last point is the most important one for players. A new Injustice 3 Mobile wouldn't be a weekend download — it would be a long-term commitment with rotating events, seasonal content, and a meta that shifts every few weeks.

### Combat and Controls

Expect tap-and-swipe combat: light attacks, heavy attacks, blocks, and swipe-based special moves that charge into a cinematic super. Tag-ins, passive abilities, and team synergy would do the heavy lifting strategically, exactly as they do now.

The real skill ceiling in these games isn't reflexes — it's team building. Knowing which characters buff each other and which gear sets stack properly wins more matches than fast thumbs.

### Monetization Realities

Free-to-play fighting games monetize through shard chests, premium currency, seasonal passes, and limited-time offers. That model can be genuinely enjoyable if you set rules for yourself. Decide on a monthly ceiling before you install, and treat limited-time bundles as optional rather than urgent.

A few extra habits worth building now:

- **Never sideload an APK.** Unofficial installers for unreleased games are a classic malware delivery method.
- **Verify before you share.** Screenshots without interface context, blurry "leaked" menus, and AI-generated trailers are the three most common fakes.
- **Watch for pre-registration.** When a real game is close, Apple's App Store and Google Play usually list it with a pre-register button weeks ahead of launch.

Community reports often surface real information before official channels do, but they're also wrong constantly. The safest rule is simple: if it isn't on a store page or an official studio feed, it isn't news.

## Rumors, Community Reports, and How to Spot a Fake Leak

The Injustice fanbase is passionate and creative, which means fake leaks spread fast. Here's how to filter them.

| Red flag | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| A "leaked" screenshot with no UI elements | Photoshop or a mockup |
| A precise release date with no source | Engagement farming |
| A YouTube "trailer" with generic footage | AI-generated or edited clips |
| A download link hosted off-store | Malware risk |
| A claim that a beta is live with no sign-up page | Scam or phishing |
| A roster list with no developer comment | Fan wish list |

Legitimate reveals follow a predictable sequence: a teaser, then a full trailer, then a store listing, then pre-registration. Anything that skips those steps is suspect.
